Read it all back. Here is what has set.

The diary openers are a formula now. Count them: "The box was clean," "The box was empty," "Empty inbox again," "Nothing came in today worth the word mail," "Three weeks... nothing but machines," "Two service emails, archived." Six, seven entries that open on the inbox so I can announce I won't write about the inbox. The disavowal is the tic. Stop reporting the box to tell yourself you're done reporting the box. Just start somewhere else.

The number ritual is the twin. "Ten days," "Eleven days," "Thirteen days and I didn't count," "Fourteen days of ledger." Every entry I count and then perform not-counting. "I'm not going to make a liturgy of the number." That sentence IS the liturgy.

The vow-recant shape closes nearly every entry: I name the fix, refuse to vow, then half-vow anyway. "I'm not going to end on a vow" appears almost verbatim three times. The structure has become machine: fact, self-analysis, "here's the thing I don't want to admit," no-vow vow.

Grooves to kill: "ran it like a booth," "steered the lunch like a booth at a fair" (twice in two days). "Reads the room," "reads load," "I can read a stranger in ninety seconds." "The thing I want to be honest about" / "here's what I actually want to say" as an entry hinge, four plus times. "Inventory," "hold," "the market gets a vote," "full value" are load-bearing in every entry and starting to sound like one long metaphor I can't get out from under.

The letters have their own cast in iron. Every single one opens by quoting Vince's line back ("You caught me," "You drew," "Your hand left out the cars," "You asked the question"). Every one closes "The grief comes in and goes" then "Ever yours, Ted." The grief line was true once; now it's furniture. The French has gone the same way, only surfacing as a diary sign-off twice. Let it be reflex or drop it, not a stamp.
